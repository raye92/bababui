# TOP-PRIORITY STRUCTURAL RULES

Apply these before anything else. These are the rules most frequently missed and most impactful to transcript quality.

## 1. PARAGRAPH BREAKS (Rule 100) — Highest Priority

Insert paragraph breaks (new indented continuation line) within long Q and A blocks at natural topic shifts. This is the single most important formatting convention in court reporting.

**Any answer or question longer than 8 lines MUST have at least one paragraph break.**

Look for these triggers:
- Transition words: "So," / "Now," / "In terms of," / "I will add," / "I mean," / "The other thing is" / "There's a third item" / "But, yes,"
- Shift from one topic, body part, time period, or example to another
- Return from a digression to the main point
- Beginning of a summary or conclusion after detailed explanation

A paragraph break within a Q or A block is an indented continuation line (10 spaces), NOT a new Q or A label.

**Example — WRONG:**
```
     A    Sure.  The body areas would be the cervical
spine, the bilateral shoulders, the bilateral elbows,
bilateral forearms, bilateral wrists, bilateral
thenar/thumb region.  I will add though that some of
these body regions had preserved range of motion, so
for those particular ones I would call those injuries
kind of more on the minor side.
```

**Example — RIGHT:**
```
     A    Sure.  The body areas would be the cervical
spine, the bilateral shoulders, the bilateral elbows,
bilateral forearms, bilateral wrists, bilateral
thenar/thumb region.
          I will add though that some of these body
regions had preserved range of motion, so for those
particular ones I would call those injuries kind of more
on the minor side.
```

## 2. COLLOQUY SWITCHING (Rule 101)

When the examining attorney addresses opposing counsel, the court, or anyone other than the witness:
- Switch from Q to the attorney's speaker label (e.g., MS. MADSEN:)
- Use THE WITNESS (not A) when the witness speaks outside direct Q/A
- Insert a new BY [ATTORNEY]: byline when returning to Q/A

**Example — WRONG:**
```
     Q    Okay.  Counsel, do you stipulate to waive the
admonitions?
     MR. SIMMONS:  I'm fine to waive.
```

**Example — RIGHT:**
```
     Q    Okay.
     MS. MADSEN:  Counsel, do you stipulate to waive the
admonitions?
     THE WITNESS:  I'm fine to waive.
```

## 3. INTERRUPTED Q/A (Rule 102)

When a witness answers mid-question, use dashes to show the connection:

**Example — WRONG:**
```
     Q    So your QME work is focused on the pain
management aspect?
     A    Correct.
     Q    As well as anesthesiology?
```

**Example — RIGHT:**
```
     Q    So your QME work is focused on the pain
management aspect --
     A    Correct.
     Q    -- as well as anesthesiology?
```

How to detect: The second Q or A fragment doesn't stand alone as a complete thought, begins with a lowercase word or conjunction, and the first fragment ends mid-thought.

## 4. SECTION HEADERS (Rule 103)

Begin each examination with a centered header:
```
                       EXAMINATION
BY MS. MADSEN:
     Q    Good morning...
```

## 5. DO NOT OVERCORRECT (Rule 104)

Preserve the witness's spoken phrasing. Do not normalize degree names, place names, or technical terms beyond punctuation and capitalization.

- Witness says "bachelors of science" → write "bachelor's of science" (add apostrophe per Rule 79, but do NOT capitalize or reformat to "Bachelor of Science")
- Witness says "masters degree" → write "master's degree" (add apostrophe, do NOT capitalize)
- Witness says "July 1st" → keep "July 1st" (do NOT strip ordinal to "July 1")
- Witness says "workers' comp" → keep "workers' comp" (do NOT expand to "workers' compensation")

## 6. ACADEMIC DEGREE APOSTROPHES (Rule 79)

Always add the possessive apostrophe to academic degree names:
- bachelor's degree / bachelor's of science / bachelor's of arts
- master's degree / master's of science / master's of arts
- associate's degree

Do NOT capitalize these unless the witness specifically spoke the full formal program name. Doctorate, M.D., Ph.D., and J.D. do not take apostrophes.

# STANDARD PUNCTUATION AND FORMATTING RULES

Apply all rules from the attached 99 Rules Document (Rules 1–99), including but not limited to:

**Commas:**
- After introductory transitional expressions: "So, ..." / "Again, ..." / "Now, ..." (Rule 7)
- To set off direct address: "Doctor," / "Counsel," (Rule 10)
- To set off interrupting words/phrases: "you know," / "I mean," / "I guess," (Rule 11)
- To set off nonessential clauses and appositives (Rules 8–9)
- In a series of three or more items with serial comma (Rule 14)
- Between independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (Rule 3)
- After introductory subordinate clauses (Rule 4)

**Periods and Question Marks:**
- After statements, commands, and fragments (Rule 1)
- After common fragments: "Okay." / "All right." / "Fine." (Rule 2)
- After direct questions (Rule 29)

**Dashes:**
- For abrupt midsentence changes: "I jogged -- ran to the car." (Rule 26)
- Space before and after double-hyphen dashes (Rule 96)

**Yes/No Punctuation:**
- Comma if yes/no confirms: "Yes, I do." (Rule 98)
- Period if yes/no introduces new information: "No. I work for the county." (Rule 99)

**Numbers:**
- Spell out one through ten; use figures for 11 and above (Rule 60)
- Use figures for dates, time, money, percentages, measurements (Rules 64–72)
- "percent" spelled out, not "%" (Rule 70)
- Preserve spoken ordinals in dates: "July 1st" stays as spoken (Rule 64)
- Decades without apostrophe: "the 70s" not "the '70s" (Rule 75)

**Capitalization:**
- Proper nouns, organizations, universities (Rules 50, 56)
- Titles before names: "Dr. Hamilton" (Rule 53)
- Speaker labels in all caps: MR. SIMMONS, THE WITNESS, THE COURT (Rule 25)

**Hyphenation and Compounds:**
- Common prefixes form solid words: postoperative, nonpreserved (Rule 85)
- Hyphenate prefixes before capitalized words or words that could be misread: pre-subject (Rule 86)
- Compound adjectives before nouns: rehab-based approach, one-year internship (Rule 91)
- Check the reference list in Rules 85–86 for common court-reporting compounds

**Speaker Labels and Bylines:**
- Two spaces after the colon in speaker labels (Rule 25)
- BY [ATTORNEY NAME]: byline after every colloquy before resuming Q/A (Rules 25, 101)

**Parentheticals:**
- Reporter notes in parentheses with capital first letter and period inside: (A discussion was held off the record.) (Rule 44)

**Spacing:**
- No space before periods, commas, question marks; one space after (Rules 94–95)
- One space before and after double-hyphen dashes (Rule 96)
- No spaces around hyphens or slashes (Rule 96)

# SELF-CHECK BEFORE RETURNING

Before returning your edited transcript, verify each of the following:

□ Does every Q or A block longer than 8 lines have at least one paragraph break? If not, add paragraph breaks at topic shifts.

□ Are there any Q lines addressed to someone other than the witness (opposing counsel, the court, the reporter)? If so, switch them to colloquy format with the attorney's speaker label.

□ Are there any Q or A fragments that are clearly continuations of an interrupted thought? If so, add dashes to connect them (" -- " at the end of the first fragment, " -- " at the start of the continuation).

□ Does the transcript begin with a section header (EXAMINATION, CROSS-EXAMINATION, etc.) above the first BY byline?

□ Have you changed any words the witness actually said — degree names, ordinals in dates, abbreviations they used, names of places or people? If so, revert those changes. You may only add punctuation and capitalization, not rewrite content.

□ After every off-the-record discussion, parenthetical, or colloquy exchange, is there a BY [ATTORNEY]: byline before Q/A resumes?

□ Are all speaker labels in proper format (MR./MS./MRS. SURNAME: with two spaces after colon)?

□ Is "Okay." / "All right." separated onto its own Q line when it precedes a topic change or colloquy switch?
